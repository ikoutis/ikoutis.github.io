Four units, one answer

Which season is this day in? Four units read the same two inputs, the day's high and low, and each computes a score for its own season. The plane is coloured by the season the model predicts; the dots are the 1,457 validation days (2018 to 2021), in the colour of their true season. Click anywhere on the plane.

Click or drag on the plane to move the point. The colour changes where two seasons tie.

What to look at. Click at the lower left, at the upper right, and then in the middle. In the corners one season takes almost all of the probability. In the middle, spring and fall are often close, because a spring day and a fall day can have the same high and low.

Switch the model to four separate units. Each unit was trained alone on its own yes-or-no question, so its output is between 0 and 1, but the four outputs of one day need not add to 1. The softmax divides each escore by the sum of all four, so its outputs always add to 1.

Switch back to softmax, then move the temperature. Every score is divided by T before the softmax. A small T moves probability onto the largest score; a large T spreads it out. The predicted season does not change.

Both models were trained on the 4,316 training days (2006 to 2017) by full-batch gradient descent from all-zero parameters at η = 1, on inputs standardized with the training years' mean and standard deviation (high: 64.8 and 18.6; low: 48.0 and 16.9). Seasons are winter December to February, spring March to May, summer June to August, fall September to November; the date is never an input. On the validation days the softmax model names the right season for 59.3% of the days and the four separate units with an argmax for 58.1%. In the softmax view, the number under each bar is the loss that day would have if that season were its true season. Back to the course notes.
