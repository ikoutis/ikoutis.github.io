A few days stand in for all of them

Newark airport, the 4,316 training days from 2006 to 2017: each day's low against its high, red for the warm half of the year and grey for the cold half. A unit trained on all of them puts its line in one place. How close does a handful of days get to it?

What to look at. The black line is where a unit trained on all 4,316 training days puts its boundary. Each red line comes from a unit trained on one batch alone: only the days that are lit up. Draw a few batches with k = 8, then with k = 64, then k = 1024. The red lines gather around the black one as k grows. The strip under the controls shows how many of all 4,316 days each batch's line gets right.

A small batch already finds roughly the right line. That is why a gradient computed from a few days is a usable estimate of the gradient computed from all of them.

What to look at. Each round uses only the next days, lit up, to compute the gradient, and moves the red line by one step. On the map, the red arrow is the step taken from the batch; the black arrow is the step all 4,316 days would have asked for from the same place. In the first rounds, with k = 32, the two arrows are a few degrees apart: a small batch knows which way is downhill. With k = 1 the red arrow can point almost anywhere.

Near the bottom the black arrow shrinks to almost nothing, and the red arrows scatter around it. No single batch is right, but their pulls average out to the pull of all the days. Press finish the epoch a few times: the line keeps drifting toward the dashed one, and after about twenty epochs it lies on it. One pass through all the batches is an epoch.

The inputs pass through the filter of this meeting's first act: each temperature is standardized with the training years' mean and standard deviation (high: 64.8 and 18.6; low: 48.0 and 16.9). Lines are drawn and written in degrees. In view 1, “trained on the batch alone” means 300 full-batch steps on those k days from all-zero parameters at η = 1; a batch whose days all come from one half of the year cannot place a line. In view 2 the days are shuffled at the start of each epoch and taken in groups of k, so every day is used once per epoch. The map shows the two weights, coloured by the loss on all 4,316 days (pale is low) with the bias at its final value; the cross marks the all-days unit.
