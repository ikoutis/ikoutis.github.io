The loss landscape

A two-input unit has two weights once the threshold is held. Its loss is then a number for every point of the plane — a surface. Turn it, stand on it, and feel the slope.

Colour and height are the same thing, the loss: pale where it is low, dark red where it is high. The map on the floor is the surface seen from above. The dot is where you stand; the arrow is the direction in which the ground falls fastest from there, which is minus the gradient. “Step downhill” moves against the gradient by the learning rate: the new position is the old one minus η times the slope. Raise η far enough and a step crosses the valley and lands higher than it started, which the log records as (up). On the AND landscape the threshold is held at θ = 1.5; on the weather landscape the bias is held at b = −12, with the daily high and the daily low as the two inputs.
