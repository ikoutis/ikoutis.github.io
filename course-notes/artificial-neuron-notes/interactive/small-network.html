Three units, two layers

One unit draws one line. This network has two units in a first layer, which read the inputs, and one unit in a second layer, which reads the outputs of the first two. The left plane shows what the first layer reads; the small plane on the right shows what the second layer reads.

What to look at. Three units were trained separately, each on its own truth table: OR, NAND and AND. They are wired so that the AND unit reads the outputs of the other two. Move the slider: it sets how many steps each part was trained for.

With few steps, the parts' outputs are far from 0 and 1. On the small plane the four rows sit near the middle, and the AND unit, which was trained on exact 0 and 1 inputs, puts some on the wrong side of its line. Find the smallest number of steps at which the network is right on all four rows, and compare it with the number of steps at which every part passes its own table.

What to look at. Now nothing is trained separately. The network is given the XOR table only, and gradient descent changes all nine parameters to lower one loss. Press run. In successful runs the loss drops, and on the small plane one line eventually separates the rows labelled 1 from the rows labelled 0. Often the rows labelled 1 end up between the two lines, as in view 1, but not always.

Press new start and run again, several times. Of the first hundred starts on this page, 22 still misclassify rows after 3,000 steps, most with two outputs near one half; start 2 is one. When a start does reach XOR, read the table: the hidden units are usually not OR and NAND. Nobody told them what to compute.

What to look at. The same three units, on 4,316 real days. The question is yes-or-no: is this day in spring or fall? Those days (red) lie between the winter days and the summer days (grey), as XOR's rows labelled 1 lie between the other two. Press run. On start 1, little happens for about a hundred steps; then the two lines move apart to the two sides of the red days.

Switch to one unit and run again. One line cannot enclose a middle band. With three units, on the small plane, look at where the first layer sends the days: the second layer only has to separate one corner.

model
Every unit is a sigmoid unit and every loss is the cross-entropy of meeting 4. View 1 trains each part as meeting 6 did: full-batch gradient descent from all-zero parameters at η = 4. Views 2 and 3 start every parameter at a random value between −1 and 1 and use full-batch gradient descent, at η = 4 on the four rows and at η = 2 on the 4,316 training days (2006 to 2017); the single unit of view 3 starts from all-zero parameters. In view 3 the inputs are standardized with the training years' mean and standard deviation, the label is 1 for days in March to May and September to November, and “validation” is the 1,457 days of 2018 to 2021.
