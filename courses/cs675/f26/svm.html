The widest street

A support vector classifier puts the widest possible street between two classes, with the decision boundary down the middle, and keeps every point off it. Drag the points. Then let a few of them onto the street, at a price.

Drag a point to move it · click on empty space to add a point of the chosen class · shift-click a point to remove it.

What to look at. Drag a point that is not ringed: the street does not move. Drag a ringed one, a support vector, and the whole street turns. Only the points on the curb matter; the rest could be deleted and nothing would change.

Choose one stray point. Without slacks the street must squeeze past it: narrow and tilted, decided by a single point. Switch slacks on. The stray point may now stand on the street, or across it, and pays a fine of C per unit of trespass ζ. At a small C the fine is cheap, the street stays wide and the point is ignored; raise C and the street narrows again to avoid paying.

Choose overlap. Without slacks there is no street at all; with them there is always one. Move C across its range and watch the trade: a wide street with many trespassers, or a narrow one with few. The two Newark presets are the same story in degrees Fahrenheit; there the weights are small numbers, so the interesting prices are the small ones, below 0.01.

the boundary
The classifier solves min ½‖w‖² + C Σ ζi subject to yi(w·xi + b) ≥ 1 − ζi and ζi ≥ 0. The curbs are the lines w·x + b = ±1, so the street is 2/‖w‖ wide, and a point's slack is ζi = max(0, 1 − yi(w·xi + b)), the hinge loss of section 7.6; a slack of 1 puts the point on the boundary. Without slacks (C = ∞) the street exists only when the classes are linearly separable, and the page then solves it exactly: in the plane the widest street rests on two points or on three, so every such candidate is tried and the widest valid one kept. With slacks the dual is solved by Platt's SMO with the linear kernel; a support vector is a point whose dual weight is not zero. The notes write the objective as ‖w‖² + C Σ ζi, without the half; the street is the same up to a rescaling of C. The Newark presets are 60 days each, drawn at random from the training years 2006–2017 of the daily high and low at Newark Liberty airport (NOAA GHCN-Daily, station USW00014734), in degrees Fahrenheit.
