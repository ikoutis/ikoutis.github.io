The loss landscape

A line y = ax + b has two parameters. Its mean squared error is then a number for every point of the (a, b) plane — a surface. Turn it, stand on it, and feel the slope.

Colour and height are the same thing, the loss: pale where it is low, dark red where it is high. The map on the floor is the surface seen from above. The dot is where you stand; the arrow is the direction in which the ground falls fastest from there, which is minus the gradient. A step downhill moves you by −ρ∇L; try a step size too large for the raw Newark landscape and watch what happens.
