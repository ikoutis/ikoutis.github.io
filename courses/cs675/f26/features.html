A feature the line was missing

Linear regression fits a line and logistic regression draws one. Both fail on data that bends. Give them one more feature and the same algorithm, unchanged, succeeds: the model is still a line, in a space with one more coordinate.

What to look at. The daily high at Newark against the day of the year. With x alone, least squares draws the best straight line through a year of weather, and it explains 6% of the variation: a line has no way to go up in July and down in January.

Add x²: a parabola, 70%, but it cannot make December look like January. Now untick the powers and tick sin and cos of the day of the year: 78%, and the curve closes on itself. Nothing changed in the algorithm. Each point simply arrived with two more coordinates, and in that three-dimensional space the data does lie close to a plane.

The prediction is b + w·φ(x): linear in the parameters w, whatever φ does to x. That is why the same gradient descent trains all of these.

What to look at. Is this day in spring or fall? Spring and fall days sit in a band between the winter days and the summer days. One logistic neuron on (high, low) draws one line, and no line picks out a band: it is right on about half of the validation days, a coin flip.

Tick x₁², x₂² and x₁x₂. The same neuron, trained the same way, is now right on about three quarters of the days. Its boundary in the plane is a curve, but in the six-dimensional space of the features it is still a single line, one weight per feature.

Try the two rings of the notes' section 4.7: with x₁² + x₂² as the one extra feature, the boundary is the circle of radius 1/√2 the notes describe. For the XOR cloud, the missing feature is the product.

Regression: the fit minimizes the mean squared error exactly, by the normal equations on standardized features, which is where gradient descent would also end up; R² is 1 minus the ratio of the model's MSE to the variance of y (notes, section 3.8). Classification: one logistic neuron, p = σ(b + w·φ(x)), trained on the negative log-likelihood by Newton's method with a small ridge so that separable data keeps finite weights. Weather: the training years are 2006–2017 (4,383 days), the validation years 2018–2021 (1,461 days, the dots on the plane); spring is March to May and fall September to November, and the date is never an input. The data: daily high and low at Newark Liberty airport, 2006–2024, from NOAA's GHCN-Daily record for station USW00014734.
